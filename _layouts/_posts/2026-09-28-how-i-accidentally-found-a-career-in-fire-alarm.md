---
title: "How I Accidentally Found a Career in the Fire Alarm Industry"
description: "How a part-time job at a fire safety company unexpectedly turned into a career in fire alarm systems, troubleshooting and programming."
date: 2026-09-28
category: "Career · From the Field"
---

When I was 22 years old, I was working nights at a pizza shop while going to school full time. It was mid-July when I decided that I needed a part-time job to better utilize my daytime hours. So, I found an opening at a local fire safety company.

My first job there was regular shop work and learning to fill fire extinguishers. I quickly learned how to fill extinguishers during my last semesters in college. I graduated in December and had a conversation with the owner. I told him that I didn't see myself servicing and inspecting fire extinguishers for the rest of my career, and he agreed.

He asked me if I wanted to learn how to inspect and service fire alarm systems.

**You could say the rest is history.**

When I first broke into the industry, I knew nothing—and that might have worked to my advantage.

I didn't have anyone growing up to show me how to fix or troubleshoot things. Couple that with my bachelor's degree in Business Administration, and I wasn't sure that this was going to be a career for me.

The first full summer of doing fire alarm inspections was a long one, and it introduced me to the busiest time of a fire alarm technician's entire year: **school vacation.**

I spent a lot of time doing inspections at various school districts in the area and was writing up a lot of deficiencies that I had no clue where to begin with.

After the summer was over, I had another conversation with the president of the company I first worked for. I expressed my interest in learning the troubleshooting and programming aspects of the job.

After a few months of in-the-field training, I was progressing in my troubleshooting efforts. I particularly enjoyed chasing ground faults—something that not a lot of technicians enjoy.

After spending time troubleshooting, I moved to a bigger company and was immediately sent to programming classes.

**As soon as I began programming, I knew this was the right choice for me.**

I had spent some time in college deciding what I wanted to do after graduation. I took some programming classes, and I was able to follow the concepts, but the execution was where I was losing it.

I didn't see the big picture.

I was asked to write a program to make a stick figure walk across the screen. My immediate thought was:

> "Why am I making this stick figure walk across the screen?"

When I was sent to fire alarm programming classes, I was finally able to answer that question—but now it was in terms of fire alarms: **What associated output will occur when a specific input is initiated?**

Suddenly, programming had a real-world purpose.

Looking back at the beginning of my career, I wish I had the electrical knowledge to understand how circuits truly worked.

But I also believe that because I didn't have any knowledge before entering the field, it meant that I didn't have any bad habits that needed to be forgotten.

There are always a lot of "what ifs" in this world, but I'm a true believer that everything happens for a reason.

**I got into this industry for a reason that I may never truly understand.**
